Hearst History

From a single newspaper to a global media powerhouse.

1887
William Randolph Hearst takes control of the San Francisco Examiner, laying the foundation for the Hearst empire.
1895
Acquires the New York Journal, igniting the legendary circulation war with Joseph Pulitzer.
1903
Launches Motor magazine (now Car and Driver), marking Hearst’s first foray into specialty publishing.
1910s–1930s
Builds a global newspaper chain, acquires wire services, and expands into newsreels and film production.
1948
Enters television with the launch of WBAL-TV in Baltimore, the beginning of Hearst Television.
1990s
Diversifies into financial services and health data, acquiring Fitch Ratings and building Hearst Health.
Today
A $12B+ privately held media and information company with 360+ brands across 40+ countries.
